Why reuse rainwater?
Reusing rainwater helps conserve a precious natural resource, reducing the demand on municipal water supplies and lowering utility bills. It also mitigates stormwater runoff, which can cause erosion and flooding. Rainwater is typically free of many contaminants found in groundwater, making it ideal for irrigation and other non-potable uses. By reusing rainwater, communities can enhance water security, promote sustainability, and reduce their environmental footprint.

How can I collect more rainwater?
To collect more rainwater, start by installing rain barrels or tanks under your roof's downspouts. Ensure your gutters are clean and well-maintained to maximize water flow. Consider using larger storage containers or connecting multiple barrels for increased capacity. You can also install a rainwater diverter to direct overflow into additional barrels. For larger-scale collection, use cisterns or underground tanks. Incorporating permeable surfaces and landscaping features like swales can also help capture and direct rainwater.

What are the benefits of rainwater?
Rainwater harvesting offers numerous benefits, including reducing reliance on municipal water supplies and lowering water bills. It conserves water, reduces stormwater runoff, and helps prevent erosion. Rainwater is naturally soft, free of chemicals like chlorine, making it better for plants and gardens. It also supports water self-sufficiency and can be a valuable resource during droughts. Additionally, rainwater systems can increase property value and contribute to environmental sustainability.

Biodiversity
What is “wild on purpose”?
“Wild on purpose” refers to the intentional practice of allowing natural processes to thrive without human interference. This approach aims to restore ecosystems to their natural state, promoting biodiversity and resilience. By letting nature take its course, we can create habitats that support a wide range of species, enhance ecosystem services, and improve environmental health. This concept encourages a hands-off approach to conservation, where human intervention is minimized to let natural dynamics flourish.

How do we protect biodiversity?
Protecting biodiversity involves several strategies: conserving natural habitats, promoting sustainable land and water use, and combating climate change. Efforts include establishing protected areas, restoring degraded ecosystems, and controlling invasive species. Supporting biodiversity-friendly policies and practices, such as sustainable agriculture and forestry, is crucial. Public education and community involvement also play key roles in raising awareness and encouraging actions that support biodiversity conservation.

Why is rewilding effective for biodiversity?
Rewilding is effective for biodiversity because it restores natural processes and habitats, allowing ecosystems to recover and thrive. By reintroducing native species and reducing human management, rewilding helps rebuild ecosystem diversity, structure, and resilience. This approach creates interconnected habitats that support a wide range of species, enhancing genetic diversity and ecosystem stability. Rewilding also mitigates climate change impacts by increasing carbon sequestration and reducing soil erosion.

Our vision and mission
How Rippl’ works
Rippl empowers communities to make a positive environmental impact through simple and enjoyable rainwater reuse. Our innovative system includes a compact rainwater collector designed for balconies, which captures and stores rainwater for various uses, such as watering plants. The collected water is monitored via a user-friendly app, providing real-time data and tips on maximizing water use. By participating in Rippl, individuals contribute to water conservation, enhance local biodiversity, and join a community dedicated to sustainable living. Together, we create ripples of change for a greener future.
